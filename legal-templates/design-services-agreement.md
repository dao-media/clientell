## 1. Scope of Work · [CORE]

1.1. Designer will provide the services and deliverables described in **Exhibit A (Statement of Work)**.

1.2. **Anything not listed in Exhibit A is out of scope.** Out-of-scope requests are handled under Section 2 (Changes).

1.3. **Revisions.** The SOW specifies the number of included revision rounds per deliverable ([e.g., 2 rounds]). A "round" is one consolidated set of feedback. Additional rounds are billed at Designer's hourly rate of **$65/hour**.

## 2. Changes & Additional Work · [CORE]

2.1. Either Party may request changes. No change is binding until described in writing and approved by both Parties (email is sufficient).

2.2. Approved changes that increase time or cost are billed at **$65/hour** or a new agreed flat fee, and may extend the timeline.

## 3. Fees & Payment Schedule · [CORE]

3.1. **Total project fee:** $[AMOUNT] (or as itemized in Exhibit A), exclusive of third-party costs (Section 8) and applicable taxes. Projects are **custom-priced to spec as a flat fee**, not hourly. Designer's hourly rate of **$65/hour** applies **only to overages** — additional revision rounds (1.3), out-of-scope or additional work (Section 2), and support beyond the warranty window (Section 13).

3.2. **Deposit — 35%.** A deposit of **35% of the total fee ($[AMOUNT])** is due after the initial consultation and **before any work begins.** This deposit reserves Designer's time and is **non-refundable** except as stated in Section 6. The deposit is credited against the final balance.

3.3. **Deposit timing controls the schedule.** The project start date and timeline in Exhibit A are contingent on timely receipt of the deposit. Specifically:

  - **Days 1–10 (calendar days, including weekends):** Deposit received in this window secures the start date estimated in Exhibit A.
  - **After Day 10:** Designer can no longer guarantee an immediate start. The project enters Designer's regular queue and will begin when capacity allows, given other active clients and onboarding. The timeline shifts accordingly.
  - **After Day 21 (3 weeks):** Designer will treat the project as **not moving forward** and may release the reserved capacity. This is **not** a cancellation penalty — Client may re-engage at any time, and Designer will resume from where things left off, subject to then-current availability and pricing.

3.4. **Remaining balance.** The remaining **65%** is invoiced [per the milestones in Exhibit A / upon completion, before final delivery/handoff]. *(Recommended for larger projects: split into a mid-project milestone payment so you're never carrying more than ~one phase of unpaid work.)*

3.5. **Delivery on payment.** Final files, source assets, production access, and transfer of ownership (Section 9) occur **only after payment in full.** Designer may withhold delivery and suspend work on any past-due balance.

3.6. **Late payments (final invoice).** Final invoices are **due within 7 days**. A **grace period runs through day 14**; any balance still unpaid **after day 14** accrues a late fee of **1.5% per month** (or $[50], whichever is greater) on the outstanding amount until paid. If a balance remains **unpaid after 28 days**, Designer may refer the debt to a **collection agency** (with collection costs borne by Client under Section 3.7). *(1.5%/mo is the freelance market norm, and late-fee terms must be in the signed contract to be enforceable — a fee added only on the invoice generally is not.)*

3.7. **Costs of collection.** If Designer must pursue an unpaid balance, Client agrees to reimburse all reasonable costs of collection, **including attorney's fees, collection-agency fees, and court costs.** *(Attorney's fees generally are NOT recoverable unless the contract says so — this clause is what makes enforcement economical.)*

3.8. **[MODULE — Personal guarantee, for entity clients on larger projects]** The individual signing on behalf of Client personally guarantees payment of all amounts due under this Agreement. *(Use this when the client is an LLC/corp and the fee is significant, so you can pursue the person if the entity dissolves or goes dark.)*

## 4. Client Responsibilities · [CORE]

4.1. A project's success depends on the Client as much as the Designer. Client agrees to:

  - Respond to questions, feedback requests, and approvals within **[3] business days**.
  - Provide all required materials (copy, images, logos, brand assets, access credentials) in usable form by the dates in Exhibit A.
  - Make available **one optional check-in call per week**, scaling to additional calls when the work calls for it.
  - Designate **one** primary point of contact with authority to give approvals.

4.2. **Client-caused delays.** If Client's delay in responding, approving, or supplying materials holds up the work, the timeline extends by at least the length of the delay, and Designer isn't responsible for missed dates caused by it.

4.3. **Dormancy / going quiet.** If Client is unresponsive for **[14] consecutive days** without prior arrangement, Designer may pause the project and reassign the reserved time. After **[30] days** of silence, Designer may treat the project as inactive; restarting may require a **re-engagement fee of $[AMOUNT]** and a new timeline. Work already completed remains billable. *(This mirrors your deposit logic and closes the most common freelancer gap — the client who vanishes mid-project.)*

## 5. Approvals & Acceptance · [CORE]

5.1. Work proceeds through **documented check-ins and written approvals** at the stages listed in Exhibit A. Client's written sign-off (email is sufficient) on a stage authorizes Designer to build on it.

5.2. A deliverable is **accepted** when Client approves it in writing or fails to submit written revisions within **[5] business days** of delivery.

5.3. Because the work is guided continuously by Client's own feedback and approvals, accepted work reflects choices Client participated in and signed off on.

## 6. Refunds · [CORE]

6.1. **Design fees are non-refundable.** The process is built on frequent check-ins and client approvals, so the output is directed by Client — in part or in whole — at each stage. There is no point at which a finished deliverable should arrive as a surprise.

6.2. **Good-faith adjustment.** If Designer was genuinely unable to deliver a contracted item, or a promised element of scope went unfulfilled, Designer will discuss a reduction of the **remaining balance by up to 25%.** This is a discretionary good-faith accommodation, is capped at 25% of the remaining (unpaid) balance, and is **not** an admission of fault or a waiver of any term of this Agreement.

## 7. Cancellation & Termination · [CORE]

7.1. **Client cancellation.** Client may cancel in writing at any time. Client remains responsible for (a) the non-refundable deposit, (b) all work performed through the cancellation date, and (c) any non-cancelable third-party costs already committed on Client's behalf (Section 8).

7.2. **Designer termination.** Designer may terminate for non-payment, dormancy (Section 4.3), abusive conduct, or Client's material breach, and will be paid for work performed to that point.

7.3. Sections that by their nature should survive termination — payment, ownership/IP, confidentiality, liability, indemnity, dispute resolution — survive.

## 8. Third-Party Costs (Hosting, Tools, Subscriptions) · [CORE]

Some projects require paid third-party services (e.g., web hosting, premium plugins, stock assets, software subscriptions). Client chooses **one** option in Exhibit A:

8.1. **Option A — Designer procures, Client reimburses.** Designer purchases needed services and lists them on an itemized final invoice, **in addition to** the quoted project fee. A handling fee of **[10–15]%** applies to pass-through costs. Any single item over **$[100]** requires Client's prior written approval.

8.2. **Option B — Client provides a controlled payment method.** Client supplies a payment method for project purchases; **every purchase is pre-approved in writing** before it's made. To keep this safe for both sides, Client is encouraged to issue a **virtual card** (e.g., via **Privacy.com**) that is **merchant-locked and spend-limited** — so the card only works at the intended vendor, up to a set cap, and can be paused or closed anytime. Issue one locked card per vendor.

8.3. **Account ownership & renewals.** Exhibit A states, for each third-party service, **who owns the account** and **who is responsible for renewals** after handoff. Designer is not responsible for recurring charges on accounts transferred to Client, and will not indefinitely carry subscriptions in Designer's own name on Client's behalf. *(This is the piece people forget — don't get stuck holding a client's hosting bill forever.)*

## 9. Intellectual Property & Ownership · [CORE]

9.1. **Transfer on full payment.** Upon **receipt of payment in full**, Designer assigns to Client the ownership of the final deliverables created specifically for Client under this Agreement. **Until payment in full, Designer retains all rights, and Client has no license to use the work.** *(For digital/creative work this is your real leverage — it does the job a lien can't. If they don't pay, they legally can't use what you made.)*

9.2. **Pre-existing & background IP.** Designer's pre-existing and independently developed materials — including but not limited to design systems, code libraries, components, frameworks and methodologies (including Designer's proprietary MX / Machine Experience framework), templates, and reusable tooling — **remain Designer's exclusive property.** Client receives a **perpetual, non-exclusive, non-transferable license** to use these materials solely as incorporated into the deliverables. *(This is where freelancers accidentally give away their toolkit. Don't.)*

9.3. **Third-party assets.** Fonts, stock, plugins, and other licensed third-party materials are governed by their own licenses; Client is responsible for maintaining any required licenses after handoff.

## 10. Portfolio & Promotion · [CORE]

10.1. **Designer's default rights.** Designer may display, reproduce, and describe the work — including process, drafts, and final deliverables — in Designer's **portfolio and marketing materials**, and may publicly identify Designer as the creator, for the purpose of promoting Designer's services and attracting new clients. This right survives completion and is **not** transferred by Section 9.

10.2. **[MODULE — Confidentiality / Attribution-Waiver Addendum].** If Client wants Designer to (a) keep the engagement confidential and/or (b) refrain from publicly claiming authorship (allowing Client to present the work as its own), the Parties will sign the separate **Confidentiality & Attribution Addendum**, and Client will pay the associated premium of **$[AMOUNT] / [X]% of project fee]**. There is no fixed industry rate for this — it's a negotiated premium, priced to the value of the visibility you're giving up. Without a signed addendum, Section 10.1 governs.

## 11. Confidentiality · [CORE]

11.1. Each Party will protect the other's non-public information and use it only for the project. This mutual duty does not, by itself, restrict Designer's portfolio rights under Section 10.1 — those are limited only by an executed Section 10.2 addendum.

## 12. Client Materials & Warranties · [CORE]

12.1. Client warrants that any content it provides (text, images, logos, fonts, data) is owned or properly licensed by Client, and that Designer's use of it as directed won't infringe anyone's rights.

12.2. Client will **defend and indemnify** Designer against claims arising from Client-supplied materials or from Client's use of the deliverables after handoff.

## 13. Warranty & Post-Launch Support · [CORE]

13.1. Designer will correct **defects in Designer's own work** reported within **[30] days** of final delivery, at no charge.

13.2. This does not cover changes Client (or third parties) make after handoff, new features, content updates, or breakage caused by third-party services, hosts, plugins, or platform updates. Support beyond the warranty window is billed hourly or via a separate maintenance agreement.

## 14. Independent Contractor · [CORE]

14.1. Designer is an independent contractor, not an employee, partner, or agent of Client. Designer controls the means and methods of the work, supplies own tools, and is responsible for own taxes and insurance.

## 15. Limitation of Liability · [CORE]

15.1. To the maximum extent permitted by law, Designer's total liability under this Agreement is limited to the **total fees paid by Client** for the project. Designer is not liable for indirect, incidental, or consequential damages (including lost profits or data), even if advised of the possibility.

## 16. Third-Party Services Disclaimer · [CORE]

16.1. Designer is not responsible for the performance, availability, pricing changes, or discontinuation of third-party services (hosts, platforms, APIs, plugins), or for outages or breaches on those services.

## 17. Force Majeure · [CORE]

17.1. Neither Party is liable for delays or failures caused by events beyond reasonable control (natural disasters, illness, outages, acts of government). Timelines extend for the duration of the event.

## 18. Dispute Resolution & Governing Law · [CORE]

18.1. **Governing law:** This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of **[West Virginia]**, without regard to conflict-of-laws rules.

18.2. **Good-faith first.** The Parties will first attempt to resolve any dispute directly, then through **mediation**, before litigation.

18.3. **Venue / small claims.** Unresolved disputes may be brought in the state courts located in **[COUNTY], West Virginia**. Smaller claims may be pursued in the appropriate limited-jurisdiction (magistrate) court up to that court's statutory limit — *confirm the current West Virginia cap before relying on it.*

## 19. General · [CORE]

19.1. **Entire agreement.** This Agreement plus Exhibit A (and any signed Modules/Addenda) is the complete agreement and supersedes prior discussions.

19.2. **Amendments** must be in writing and signed by both Parties.

19.3. **Severability.** If any provision is held invalid, the rest remains in effect.

19.4. **Waiver.** Not enforcing a term once doesn't waive it later.

19.5. **Assignment.** Neither Party may assign this Agreement without the other's written consent, except Designer may use subcontractors while remaining responsible for the work.

19.6. **Electronic signatures** are valid and binding.
